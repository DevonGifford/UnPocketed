# Which provider ships first?

Research for [ticket 04](../issues/04-which-provider-ships-first.md). All provider figures
retrieved from primary vendor documentation on **2026-10-07**. Prices, caps and model names
change; re-verify before relying on any number here.

Builds on [ticket 01's research](01-provider-upload-limits.md), whose 2 GB / 25 MB / ASH 7.2K
findings are **re-confirmed below against the live docs**, not inherited.

## Verdict

**PR7 ships AssemblyAI. PR8 ships Deepgram, plus `custom-openai.ts` behind a declared capability
cap. Chunking is out of v0.1 entirely.**

The ticket's conclusion — Deepgram first, because it "removes a whole workstream" and is the
genuinely different API shape — is **half right, and the half that is wrong changes the order**.

Three findings drive this:

1. **The candidate pool was drawn too small.** The ticket assumes only Deepgram clears a
   two-hour recording from a serverless client. **AssemblyAI also does**, at **2.2 GB** by direct
   byte upload, and its upload URLs are explicitly *not* publicly reachable — so §3.1 is
   satisfied, not merely tolerated. There are two chunk-free providers, not one. Chunking is
   therefore deletable from v0.1 under *several* orderings, which frees the first-provider
   decision to be made on other grounds.

2. **Deepgram cannot implement §21's state machine.** Its docs state that *"Deepgram does not
   store transcripts, so the API response is the only opportunity to retrieve the transcript."*
   §3.1 blocks the callback that would decouple result retrieval. So on Android, a two-hour
   upload whose HTTP response is lost — app killed, network changed, Doze — loses the transcript
   **permanently, with no server-side state to query on relaunch**. §21's `Transcribing` is then
   a local fiction: the recording is either stuck in it forever or reset to `Failed` and
   re-uploaded at ~90 MB and re-paid. AssemblyAI's `queued / processing / completed / error`
   maps ~1:1 onto §21's four states, and a persisted job id makes `Transcribing` survive app
   death. This is the difference between §21 being implementable and being aspirational on
   exactly §34's two-hour target.

3. **The interface asymmetry settles the ordering.** A `TranscriptionProvider` designed around an
   async job trivially accommodates a synchronous provider — the job completes immediately, zero
   polls. The reverse is false. Transcripts persist in SQLite from PR7 onward, so designing
   against the harder case first avoids precisely the migration [01](01-provider-upload-limits.md)
   warned about. Shipping Deepgram first buys a simpler PR7 and pays for it with an interface
   change at PR8, after rows exist.

Against this, Deepgram's onboarding is genuinely better ($200 free credit vs $50) and it retains
neither audio nor transcript, which is a §26 advantage. Both are real costs of this decision and
are recorded as accepted in [§7](#7-what-this-decision-costs).

**What "removes a whole workstream" actually deletes:** audio *slicing* — and it is deleted by
either provider in the pair, so the map's contingent chunk-assembly fog entry **evaporates
rather than graduates**. It does **not** delete long-upload-from-a-phone: neither provider
offers resumable upload, and AssemblyAI's documented strategy is whole-file retry. See
[§5](#5-the-tickets-argument-attacked).

## 1. The constraint that defines the candidate pool

§3.1 forbids Unpocketed from making audio publicly reachable or from hosting an inbound
endpoint. That rules out, as established in 01 and unchanged today: Groq's `url` parameter and
Batch API, and Deepgram's `callback`. It does **not** rule out a provider-hosted upload target
that the client writes to directly, because the app is the uploader and no Unpocketed server
exists in the path.

That distinction is what the ticket's input list misses, and it is load-bearing. **AssemblyAI's
`/v2/upload` is exactly that shape**, and AssemblyAI documents that the resulting URL is private:

Source: [Why can't I access recording URLs from the /upload endpoint directly?](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/faq/why-cant-i-access-urls-from-the-upload-endpoint-directly)
(retrieved 2026-10-07), verbatim:

> The URLs generated by AssemblyAI's /upload endpoint are designed exclusively for transcript
> processing and are only accessible by our API. These URLs cannot be accessed directly, which is
> why you receive an "AccessDenied" error when you try to navigate to the URLs or use them in
> other applications.

The page then recommends S3/GCS/Azure *only* for users who actually want a public URL — which
Unpocketed does not. So the audio is uploaded by the device, over an authenticated request, to
storage only the provider can read. This is categorically different from Groq's `url`, which
requires *us* to publish the audio somewhere fetchable.

**Pool surveyed, honestly scoped:** Groq, OpenAI and Deepgram were carried forward from 01.
AssemblyAI was added and cleared the bar. **ElevenLabs Scribe, Speechmatics and Gladia were not
examined** — the pool stopped once a provider that satisfied every constraint was found. Do not
read this document as an exhaustive market survey; a cheaper or better-shaped provider may exist
among those three.

Also ruled out without fetching, on the same §3.1 grounds as Groq's `url`: Google's long-audio
recognition and Azure's batch transcription, both of which ingest from cloud blob URIs.

## 2. The numbers, per provider

Two hours of audio at the project's **measured** encoder output — ~96 kbps AAC in `.m4a`, not the
preset's requested 128 kbps — is **~86.4 MB, or ~90 MB including container overhead**. The 90 MB
figure is used throughout.

### Throughput limits — the axis on which Groq fails and the other two do not

| Provider | Limit shape | Over-limit behaviour | Fatal for one 2 h file? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Groq** | **ASH 7.2K** = 7,200 audio-sec/hour | `429` | **Yes — one file = 100% of the hourly allowance** |
| Deepgram | 50 concurrent (PAYG, REST) | n/a for one user | No |
| **AssemblyAI** | 5 parallel jobs (Free), 200+ (Paid) | **queued FIFO, not rejected** | No |

Groq, source: [Rate Limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits) (raw markdown:
`https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits.md`), retrieved 2026-10-07. **ASH 7.2K is
re-confirmed and unchanged**, and the page still states which tier it describes, verbatim:

> Note that the limits shown below are the base limits for the Developer plan, and higher limits
> are available for select workloads and enterprise use cases.

| MODEL ID | RPM | RPD | TPM | TPD | ASH | ASD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `whisper-large-v3` | 20 | 2K | – | – | **7.2K** | 28.8K |
| `whisper-large-v3-turbo` | 20 | 2K | – | – | **7.2K** | 28.8K |

Definitions, verbatim: `**ASH:** Audio seconds per hour`, `**ASD:** Audio seconds per day`. So
**7,200 audio-seconds per hour is exactly two hours of audio per hour, on the paid Developer
plan**, and 01's reading stands: a single §34-target recording exhausts the hourly allowance, and
§21's "A failed transcription should be retryable" is unsatisfiable for up to an hour. Chunking
does not relieve it — it is a throughput ceiling, orthogonal to size.

**Still unverified, as in 01:** whether a *single* request of 7,200 audio-seconds is rejected
outright for exceeding the whole hourly bucket or merely drains it. The docs do not say, and the
page still warns "there may be exceptions to these limits... view the current, exact rate limits
for your organization on the limits page". **Free-tier ASH/ASD remain unpublished** — the word
"free" does not appear on the rate-limits page. Under §19 the user's tier is not ours to choose,
so the app must behave correctly on the lower, undocumented tier.

AssemblyAI, source: [Rate limits](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/pre-recorded-audio/rate-limits)
(retrieved 2026-10-07), verbatim:

> When you submit audio files to the /v2/transcript endpoint, AssemblyAI processes them in
> parallel up to your account's rate limit. The rate limit is the maximum number of transcription
> jobs that can be actively processing at the same time.

| Account type | Rate limit (parallel transcriptions) |
| --- | --- |
| Free | 5 |
| Paid | 200+ |

> If you submit a transcription that would exceed your rate limit, it is added to a queue. Queued
> transcriptions are processed automatically in FIFO (first-in, first-out) order as previously
> submitted transcriptions complete.

> In addition to the parallel-job rate limit, there is an HTTP rate limit for the API that
> restricts accounts to a maximum of 20,000 requests per five minutes. This limit counts all
> requests across endpoints — submissions (POST) and polling (GET) combined — and exceeding it
> returns an HTTP 403. To stay under it, use webhooks instead of frequent polling, or widen and
> jitter your polling interval.

**Neither AssemblyAI nor Deepgram imposes an audio-throughput ceiling.** Both cap *concurrency*,
which a single-user phone app will never approach. This is the second independent reason Groq
cannot be the first provider, and it is the one chunking cannot fix.

One consequence for PR7: the 20,000-requests-per-5-minutes HTTP limit is generous, but it counts
polls. Since §3.1 forbids the webhook AssemblyAI recommends, PR7 must poll — so use a widened,
jittered interval. A 3-second poll over a two-hour job is ~2,400 requests, well inside the limit,
but it is the kind of thing that compounds across retries.

Deepgram, source: [API Rate Limits](https://developers.deepgram.com/reference/api-rate-limits)
(retrieved 2026-10-07): Nova-3 pre-recorded is **up to 50 concurrent requests** on Pay-As-You-Go,
in each of four regions (`api.deepgram.com`, `api.eu.`, `api.au.`, `api.in.`). Whisper Cloud is
**3 concurrent, North America only**. Note two live discrepancies in Deepgram's own
documentation: the pre-recorded page says **100** concurrent while the rate-limits reference says
**50** (01 recorded this; it persists), and the pricing page now says **"Up to 5 for Deepgram
Whisper Cloud"** against the reference's **3**. The reference is the more specific source.
Immaterial here — one user, one request — but it means Deepgram's published limits are not
self-consistent.

**Transcription quality was not independently verified and cannot be from vendor documentation.**

figures any of them publish against a stated metric are Groq's (WER 10.3% / 12%), which are not
comparable to AssemblyAI's or Deepgram's unpublished-methodology claims. Quality is therefore
**not** a factor this document can weigh, and the decision does not rest on it. §22's
retranscription is the project's own answer to this: it makes provider quality something the user
verifies rather than something the spec asserts, and that argues for getting *two* providers in
front of users rather than for picking the "best" one now.

## 3. The Groq conflict: still unresolved

The ticket flags this as untested. **It remains unresolved from primary sources on 2026-10-07.**
Both rows are still live on the same page, adjacent, with no reconciling text. Verbatim from
[Speech to Text](https://console.groq.com/docs/speech-to-text.md), under **Audio File
Limitations**:

> Max File Size
>
> 25 MB (free tier), 100MB (dev tier)
>
> Max Attachment File Size
>
> 25 MB. If you need to process larger files, use the `url` parameter to specify a url to the file instead.

And from **Audio Preprocessing** on the same page, a third framing that agrees with neither
cleanly:

> ...(without chunking, Groq API speech-to-text endpoints accept up to 25MB for free tier and
> 100MB for [dev tier](https://console.groq.com/settings/billing)).

**I am not resolving this.** The three statements are mutually consistent only under the reading
that "Max Attachment File Size: 25 MB" is a tier-invariant ceiling on *multipart upload* while
the 25/100 MB figures bound the *request*, reachable above 25 MB only via `url`. That is the
plain reading and the one 01 took, but the Audio Preprocessing parenthetical describes 100 MB as
what the *endpoint accepts*, which cuts the other way. **What would settle it: a real multipart
POST of a ~90 MB `.m4a` to `/v1/audio/transcriptions` with a dev-tier key.** Nothing short of
that should be trusted, and no design decision should depend on the answer.

**It does not affect this verdict**, for a reason stronger than 01's: at the project's measured
~90 MB, Groq fails on the 25 MB reading and passes only marginally (1.1x headroom) on the 100 MB
one — and even a pass would not help, because **ASH 7.2K rules Groq out independently of file
size**. The size conflict is now moot for provider *selection*. It becomes live again only if
Groq is reached through `custom-openai.ts`, which is why that adapter needs a declared cap
([§6.3](#63-custom-openaits-ships-at-pr8-behind-a-declared-cap)) rather than a guess.

A new observation: Groq's STT page now carries a **"Working with Larger Audio Files"** section
that did not feature in 01's quotes, verbatim:

> For audio files that exceed our size limits or require more precise control over transcription,
> we recommend implementing audio chunking. This process involves:
>
> 1. Breaking the audio into smaller, overlapping segments
> 2. Processing each segment independently
> 3. Combining the results while handling overlapping

It links to a Groq cookbook chunking tutorial. So **both** 25 MB providers now prescribe
client-side chunking in their own documentation — which is exactly the workstream this verdict
declines to take on, and confirms it is a real body of work rather than a few lines of code.

## 4. Response shapes, concretely

This is what question 3 turns on. The two providers in v0.1 differ on **four** independent axes,
not one — which is what makes the pair worth shipping under §3.3.

| Axis | AssemblyAI (PR7) | Deepgram (PR8) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lifecycle** | **Async job**: upload → submit → poll | **Synchronous**: one POST returns the transcript |
| **Transport** | `POST /v2/upload` (bytes) → `POST /v2/transcript` with `audio_url` (JSON) | `POST /v1/listen` with `--data-binary`, `Content-Type: audio/*` |
| **Result nesting** | **Flat**: `text` at top level | **Deep**: `results.channels[].alternatives[].transcript` |
| **Timestamp unit** | **Milliseconds** (integer) | **Seconds** (float) |

### AssemblyAI

Flow, from [Transcript Status](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/pre-recorded-audio/check-transcript-status)
(retrieved 2026-10-07): `POST /v2/transcript` returns an `id` immediately; `GET /v2/transcript/{id}`
is polled until terminal. Statuses, verbatim:

| Status | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `processing` | The audio file is being processed. |
| `queued` | The audio file is waiting to be processed. This status is only returned when a job is actually queued, such as when you've exceeded your rate limit. Otherwise, transcripts go directly to processing. |
| `completed` | The transcription has completed successfully. |
| `error` | An error occurred while processing the audio file. |

> If the transcription fails, the status of the transcript is `error`, and the transcript includes
> an `error` property explaining what went wrong.

Response, from [Get a transcript](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/pre-recorded-audio/api-reference/transcripts/get)
(retrieved 2026-10-07), abridged to the shape:

```json
{
  "id": "9ea68fd3-f953-42c1-9742-976c447fb463",
  "status": "completed",
  "speech_model": null,
  "language_model": "assemblyai_default",
  "acoustic_model": "assemblyai_default",
  "language_code": "en_us",
  "language_confidence": 0.9959,
  "audio_url": "https://assembly.ai/wildfires.mp3",
  "text": "Smoke from hundreds of wildfires in Canada is triggering air quality alerts...",
  "words": [
    { "text": "Smoke", "start": 250, "end": 650, "confidence": 0.97465, "speaker": null }
  ],
  "utterances": [ { "confidence": 0.9359, "speaker": "A", "text": "...", "words": [] } ]
}
```

- **Word timings:** yes, `words[]`, `start`/`end` in **milliseconds**.
- **Confidence:** per word, per utterance, and overall.
- **Diarisation:** `utterances[]` with `speaker`, opt-in via `speaker_labels`, **$0.02/hr add-on**.
- **§20 provenance:** `speech_model` / `language_model` / `acoustic_model` are returned, so
  "which provider and model generated this?" is answerable from the response.

```json
  },

    "channels": [ { "alternatives": [ {
      "transcript": "Yeah. As as much as, it's worth celebrating...",
      "confidence": 0.99902344,
      "words": [ { "word": "yeah", "start": 0.08, "end": 0.32, "confidence": 0.9975586, "punctuated_word": "Yeah." } ],
      "paragraphs": { "transcript": "...", "paragraphs": [ { "sentences": [ { "text": "Yeah.", "start": 0.08, "end": 0.32 } ], "num_words": 63, "start": 0.08, "end": 25.52 } ] }
    } ] } ]
  }
}
```

- **Word timings:** yes, `words[]`, `start`/`end` in **seconds** (float).
- **Confidence:** per word and per alternative.
- **Diarisation:** opt-in `diarize`; concurrency drops to 50/25 per region when enabled.
- **§20 provenance:** `metadata.model_info` gives name, version and `arch`.
- **Extraction path:** `results.channels[0].alternatives[0].transcript` — three levels of
  indexing, each of which can be absent, and `alternatives` is plural by design.

And the property that drives the verdict, verbatim from the same page:

> Deepgram does not store transcripts, so the API response is the only opportunity to retrieve the
> transcript. Save output or return transcriptions to a callback URL for custom processing.

The sentence names the two options. §3.1 removes the second. **So "save the output" is the only
one available, and it can only happen if the HTTP response arrives.**

## 5. The ticket's argument, attacked

### 5.1 "Removes a whole workstream" — which workstream, exactly

Split it, because the two halves have different answers:

- **Audio slicing: genuinely deleted.** Both AssemblyAI (2.2 GB) and Deepgram (2 GB) clear ~90 MB
  with >20x headroom. No MP4/ADTS parser, no native module, no overlap handling, no chunk
  de-duplication, no N-way partial-failure model, no chunk UI. §8's inability to slice audio
  stops being a blocker rather than being worked around. **This half of the ticket's claim is
  correct, and it survives the ordering change.**

- **Long-upload-from-a-phone: not deleted by either provider.** Neither offers resumable or
  multipart-chunked upload. AssemblyAI's own documented strategy is **whole-file retry**, from
  [Implement Retry Upload Error Logic](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/pre-recorded-audio/guides/retry-upload-error)
  (retrieved 2026-10-07): the sample loops `transcribe(file_path)` with a delay, i.e. re-uploads
  from byte zero. So a single ~90 MB POST over mobile data, with no resumability, on a device
  that may change networks or sleep mid-transfer, remains in v0.1 **whichever provider ships**.
  01 flagged this as "the failure mode nobody's size cap covers"; it is still uncovered.

  What AssemblyAI *does* remove is the compounding of that risk: upload and result retrieval are
  separate requests, so a dead poll does not lose a completed transcript, and a successful upload
  is not re-sent merely to collect a result. Under Deepgram they are the same request, so the
  whole 90 MB rides on one connection surviving long enough to also deliver the transcript.

**Net: the ticket's headline claim is overstated but not wrong.** It deletes the larger and
nastier of the two workstreams — the one that touches §10, §18 and §21 — and leaves a smaller one
that is PR-local (retry, error surfacing, maybe a wifi-only preference) and already partly §32's
and ticket 02's business.

### 5.2 Does PR8 reintroduce chunking inside v0.1?

**No — and this is the question the ticket could not answer, because it assumed the second
provider had to be a 25 MB one.** It does not. AssemblyAI and Deepgram are *both* large-cap and
*already different enough on four axes* to stress §3.3's abstraction harder than a
Deepgram + Groq pair would. The second provider stresses the adapter **without** reintroducing
the cap problem, which is exactly the possibility the ticket asked to be considered.

So: **chunking does not land in v0.1 regardless of ordering within the chosen pair.** The map's
first *Not yet specified* entry — "How `Transcript` and the transcription states represent chunk
assembly and partial failure" — is **contingent on a 25 MB provider shipping, and no 25 MB
provider ships. It evaporates rather than graduates.**

The ticket's conditional phrasing ("chunking is in v0.1 unless Deepgram ships first") should be
restated: **chunking is out of v0.1 because the pair is AssemblyAI + Deepgram.** It would return
only if someone reinstated Groq or OpenAI as a *first-class* provider, which
[§6.3](#63-custom-openaits-ships-at-pr8-behind-a-declared-cap) deliberately avoids by making them
reachable only through a capped escape hatch.

### 5.3 Is ordering the pair the other way better, given that PR7 is the thinner PR?

The ticket's framing assumed the choice was "Deepgram first or a 25 MB provider first". With the
real pair it becomes "async first or sync first", and the answer flips the ticket's instinct.

**Arguments for Deepgram first (the ticket's position):**

- Simplest possible PR7: one POST, parse JSON, done. Reaches PR7's exit condition — "An existing
  recording can produce and display a real transcript" — with the least code.
- Its deeply nested response forces the response-normalisation seam immediately, which is the
  part that must be right from transcript #1 because rows persist.
- Best onboarding: $200, no card.

**Arguments for AssemblyAI first (taken):**

- **§21's states become real rather than decorative.** `queued/processing/completed/error` maps
  onto `not-transcribed/transcribing/transcribed/failed` almost directly, and the mapping is
  *checkable* against a server. Under Deepgram, `Transcribing` exists only in local state and is
  unrecoverable after process death, because there is nothing to ask.
- **The interface superset argument.** An interface built for async jobs absorbs a sync provider
  for free; the reverse requires a breaking change at PR8, after transcripts exist in SQLite.
  This is the same migration-cost argument 01 made about `Transcript`, applied to
  `TranscriptionProvider`.
- **PR7 is the thinner PR.** §38 gives PR7 five deliverables; PR8 carries provider settings,
  model selection, secure key storage, a second provider *and* provider switching. Putting the
  harder adapter in PR7 balances the roadmap, and the extra PR7 work — persisting a job id,
  resuming a poll on launch — lands naturally under PR7's own "transcript persistence" and
  "transcription states" deliverables.
- **It is correct under both readings of Deepgram's 504.** See immediately below.

### 5.4 Deepgram's 10-minute timeout: unresolved, and the ordering is robust to it

This is the strongest single counter to Deepgram-first and **I cannot resolve it from primary
sources.** The documented sentence is:

> **Processing time**: Requests exceeding 10 minutes (Nova/Base/Enhanced) or 20 minutes (Whisper)
> return a `504: Gateway Timeout` error.

The bullet's **label** says "Processing time", implying server-side compute only. The
**sentence** says "**Requests** exceeding 10 minutes", whose plain reading is request wall-clock
— which would include upload. Deepgram publishes no clarification, and nothing elsewhere in its
docs distinguishes the two.

The stakes, if the clock includes upload: ~90 MB is ~720 Mbit, so finishing inside 10 minutes
needs **~1.2 Mbps sustained uplink**. That is not a safe assumption on mobile data. At 1 Mbps the
upload alone takes ~12 minutes and 504s before transcription begins; at 5 Mbps it takes ~2.4
minutes and is fine. So under the pessimistic reading, Deepgram fails on precisely §34's
two-hour target on exactly the networks a recording app is used on — and §3.1 blocks the callback
that Deepgram's docs offer as the fix.

**What would settle it:** POST a ~90 MB file to `/v1/listen` on a deliberately throttled
connection (sub-1 Mbps) with a real key, and see whether the 504 arrives. Until someone does
that, treat it as an open risk on Deepgram.

**Why this does not need resolving to decide:** AssemblyAI documents **no processing-time
timeout at all**, its jobs **queue rather than fail**, and the upload is a separate request from
the result. **AssemblyAI-first is correct under both readings**, so the verdict does not depend on
an unresolvable fact. Deepgram arrives at PR8 with this risk already understood and with
AssemblyAI available as the fallback — which is the right way round, rather than discovering it
in PR7 with no alternative shipped.

## 6. The decision

### 6.1 First provider: AssemblyAI (PR7)

Async job model, 2.2 GB direct upload, §3.1-clean, $0.15–0.21/hr, $50 free credits with no card,
no audio-throughput ceiling, failed jobs not billed, and a status vocabulary that makes §21
implementable. It is also cheaper per hour than Deepgram and OpenAI.

PR7 must therefore deliver, beyond §38's list: a persisted provider job id on the recording (or
on a pending transcript row), poll resumption on app launch, and a jittered poll interval.

### 6.2 Second provider: Deepgram (PR8)

Chosen because it differs on all four axes in [§4](#4-response-shapes-concretely) — synchronous
rather than job-based, raw-binary rather than upload-then-reference, deeply nested rather than
flat, seconds rather than milliseconds. If the adapter survives Deepgram, §3.3's claim that "the
rest of the application should not care" has been tested against something that genuinely does
not resemble the first provider, which two Whisper-compatible endpoints would never have done.

It is also the right *second* choice specifically because its weaknesses are survivable once a
working provider exists: if the 504 risk proves real, or a user loses a transcript to a dropped
response, Deepgram can be documented as better suited to shorter recordings without blocking the
product.

**Rejected as second provider: Groq and OpenAI.** Either would reintroduce the 25 MB cap and
therefore the chunking workstream inside v0.1, which is the trap [§5.2](#52-does-pr8-reintroduce-chunking-inside-v01)
identifies. Groq additionally fails on ASH regardless of chunking.

### 6.3 `custom-openai.ts` ships at PR8, behind a declared cap

**Yes, it is worth shipping, and it is cheap.** The Whisper-compatible shape is the de facto
standard — Groq's endpoint is literally `api.groq.com/openai/v1/audio/transcriptions`, and local
runtimes expose the same surface — so one adapter taking a base URL, a key, and a model name
makes §3.3's promise real: Groq, OpenAI, a self-hosted Whisper, or a provider that does not exist
yet, with no new code. It is also the honest answer to "we dropped Groq", since Groq is 6x
cheaper than anything in the shipping pair and some users will want it.

**But it must not inherit their chunking problem.** Ship it with a declared capability — a
`maxUploadBytes` (and `maxDurationMs`) on the provider — and a **pre-flight refusal** in the UI:
"This recording is 90 MB; the configured provider accepts 25 MB. Use a shorter recording or a
different provider." That turns a cap into a stated limitation instead of a workstream, and it is
honest in a way silent failure or a surprise 413 is not. §3.5 ("no artificial feature gates") is
not violated: the gate is the provider's, disclosed, not Unpocketed's.

Two things the UI must say when `custom-openai` points at Groq:

- The 25 MB cap applies, so long recordings will be refused — and the 25-vs-100 MB conflict in
  [§3](#3-the-groq-conflict-still-unresolved) means the true cap should be treated as 25 MB until
  tested, with the value user-editable rather than hardcoded on a guess.
- **ASH 7.2K still applies.** Even a file that fits will exhaust the hourly audio allowance at
  two hours, so §21's retry promise is unsatisfiable on Groq **regardless of file size**. This is
  the one provider where "retryable" has to be qualified in the UI.

### 6.4 Does §18's `TranscriptionProvider` survive both? No — it needs two additions

As sketched, §18 is:

```ts
interface TranscriptionProvider {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  transcribe(audio: AudioSource, options: TranscriptionOptions): Promise<TranscriptionResult>;
}
```

**What survives:** `Promise<TranscriptionResult>` is fine *functionally* for both providers.
Polling hides inside the promise; a sync provider resolves after one round trip. The ticket's
worry that a flat promise is "wrong as sketched" was premised on a 25 MB provider shipping first
and forcing progress and partial results. **That premise is gone**, so **no progress or
partial-result surface is needed in v0.1** — and adding one speculatively would be §3.6
("simple before clever") violated.

**What does not survive, and why each is necessary rather than merely available:**

1. **A capability surface.** `custom-openai.ts` cannot be shipped safely without the adapter
   declaring what it accepts, because the whole point of §6.3's pre-flight refusal is that
   application code outside the transcription layer must be able to ask "will this file go
   through?" without containing provider-specific logic — which is §18's own rule. Minimum:

   ```ts
   interface ProviderCapabilities {
     maxUploadBytes?: number;    // Deepgram 2e9, AssemblyAI 2.2e9, custom-openai user-set (default 25e6)
     maxDurationMs?: number;     // AssemblyAI 10h; others undocumented
     supportsDiarization: boolean;
   }
   ```

2. **A resumable job handle.** This is the real interface change, and it is forced by PR7, not
   PR8. A two-hour transcription outlives the app process on Android. `transcribe()` returning
   only a promise means a killed app loses the only reference to a job that is still running and
   already paid for.

   The handle has to be surfaced **the moment the provider issues it**, not on completion —
   otherwise the app dies before the caller ever learns the job ref, and resumption is
   impossible. A `resume(jobRef)` method alone does not achieve this: nothing in the interface
   would ever hand the caller a `jobRef` to pass back, because the only return value resolves
   after the job is already done. So the ref must come out through a callback on the options:

   ```ts
   interface TranscriptionOptions {
     // ...existing options
     /** Called once, as soon as the provider issues a job id. Persist it synchronously. */
     onJobRef?: (jobRef: string) => void;
   }

   interface TranscriptionProvider {
     id: string;
     name: string;
     capabilities: ProviderCapabilities;
     transcribe(audio: AudioSource, options: TranscriptionOptions): Promise<TranscriptionResult>;
     /** Re-attach to a job already in flight. Undefined for synchronous providers. */
     resume?(jobRef: string, options: TranscriptionOptions): Promise<TranscriptionResult>;
   }
   ```

   AssemblyAI calls `onJobRef` after `POST /v2/transcript` returns its `id`, then polls;
   `resume` re-enters the same poll loop from a persisted ref on launch. **Deepgram never calls
   `onJobRef` and leaves `resume` undefined** — it has no job id and nothing to re-attach to.
   That is the superset property that makes this ordering cheap: the sync provider is a free
   subset of the async interface, implementing neither member.

   **Decide this now**, as the ticket asks, because it changes what PR7's SQLite schema stores —
   and the map's "Raw `expo-sqlite`, no ORM" decision explicitly named chunk assembly as its
   revisit trigger. **That trigger does not fire**: one nullable `providerJobRef` column on a
   transcript row is not a reason to add an ORM, so the no-ORM decision stands undisturbed.

**Deliberately excluded from v0.1:** word timings, per-word confidence and diarisation. Both
providers return them and both expose diarisation as a paid add-on, but §6 makes speaker ID a
non-goal and §23 keeps the transcript screen to reading, selecting, copying, editing,
attribution, retranscription and export. **Availability is not necessity** — the argument for
enriching the stored result has to come from a requirement, and there isn't one. Note that the
map's standing decision to build v1.0's *interface* early does not extend to storing data no
screen reads.

## 7. What this decision costs

Recorded as accepted, not omitted.

1. **Worse onboarding than the alternative.** Deepgram offers $200 and no card; AssemblyAI offers
   $50 and no card. Both clear the "no card to start" bar that OpenAI fails, but a first provider
   with 4x the free credit was available and was not chosen. At $0.15/hr, $50 is ~166 two-hour
   recordings, which is ample for a user evaluating the app — so the cost is real but small. It is
   also partly offset: AssemblyAI documents that **failed jobs are not billed** ("Credits are
   deducted only after a successful transcription completes"), which Deepgram does not, so the
   $50 buys more *usable* attempts than a straight 4x comparison suggests — and it is precisely
   §21's retry path that would otherwise burn credit twice on a two-hour file.

2. **AssemblyAI retains transcripts server-side; Deepgram retains neither.** This is a genuine
   §26 regression against the rejected option, and it is the one place where the chosen provider
   is *worse on the project's own values*. Primary source, from
   [Can I delete the transcripts I have created using the API?](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/faq/can-i-delete-the-transcripts-i-have-created-using-the-api)
   (retrieved 2026-10-07), verbatim:

   > Completed transcripts are stored for 30 days by default and are automatically deleted after
   > that. You can also delete them earlier at any time using the DELETE endpoint covered on this
   > page of our documentation, or shorten the retention window by setting a time-to-live (TTL) on
   > the Data Controls page in the dashboard.

   The quote above covers *completed transcripts*. **Audio retention is the figure that matters
   more** — §3.2 makes the recording the sacred artifact — and it is shorter. Source:
   [Data retention and model training](https://assemblyai.com/docs/faq/how-long-does-aai-retain-data)
   (retrieved 2026-10-07), for the async production environment with no BAA or TTL configured:

   > Deletion process begins at 24 hours and is at most 48 hours

   — against *"begins at 30 days"* for the transcription artifacts. So the **audio is gone inside
   48 hours** and only the text persists for 30 days, which is a materially milder §26 exposure
   than the transcript figure alone suggests.

   **One mitigation does not exist on our path, and it is worth stating plainly:** zero data
   retention is offered for the **streaming** product when opted out of model training. The async
   endpoint Unpocketed would use has **no zero-retention option** — only shorter TTLs, or BAA
   terms defaulting to 72 hours. A privacy-maximising user therefore *cannot* configure their own
   AssemblyAI account to store nothing, which is exactly what Deepgram gives for free. That is the
   residual cost of this decision and it does not go away.

   The mitigations that do apply: audio deletion begins at 24 hours unprompted, transcript
   retention is bounded at 30 days with TTL presets down to 1 day, and `DELETE /v2/transcript/{id}`
   removes a transcript on demand. Under §19 this is the user's own
   account and the provider's own terms, which §19 already requires Unpocketed to disclose. **But
   §19's current wording is not sufficient** — it requires explaining that submitting audio
   "sends that recording to the selected external provider". It does not say the provider may
   *keep* it. See [§8](#8-consequences-for-the-spec).

   Note the 30-day window is also what makes the job-resumption argument sound: a job id persisted
   in SQLite stays redeemable for far longer than any plausible app-restart gap.

3. **A heavier PR7.** Upload, submit, poll, persist a job ref, resume on launch — against
   Deepgram's single POST. Mitigated by PR7 being §38's thinner PR and by the work landing under
   deliverables PR7 already owns.

4. **Quality is unverified.** The decision rests on cost, caps, limits, lifecycle and §3.1
   compliance — all documented — and **not** on accuracy, which no vendor publishes comparably.
   If AssemblyAI proves materially worse than Deepgram on real recordings, PR8 makes switching a
   settings change, and §22's retranscription lets a user compare directly. That is §3.3 working
   as intended, and it is the reason this decision is low-regret.

## 8. Consequences for the spec

Findings that invalidate or outrun something the spec already assumes.

- **§19 is incomplete, not wrong.** It requires disclosing that audio is *sent* to the provider.
  With AssemblyAI the audio is also *stored*, for up to 48 hours, and the transcript for up to 30
  days by default (see [§7](#7-what-this-decision-costs)). Add a retention sentence to the
  disclosure naming both windows, and consider surfacing the provider's TTL/DELETE controls.
  §19 should not imply the user can opt into zero retention on the async path — they cannot.
- **§19's settings mockup shows `Groq` / `whisper-large-v3-turbo`.** That illustration is now
  **actively misleading**: Groq cannot be a first-class provider in v0.1 (ASH 7.2K, and a cap it
  fails on the plain reading). The mockup should read `AssemblyAI` / `universal-2`. This is the
  specific thing the ticket suspected was "illustration rather than decision" — it was
  illustration, and it should change.
- **§18's example interface is insufficient** on two counts, both decided in
  [§6.4](#64-does-18s-transcriptionprovider-survive-both-no--it-needs-two-additions): no
  capability surface and no resumable job handle. It needs no progress or partial-result surface.
- **§20 is satisfied by both providers.** AssemblyAI returns `speech_model`/`language_model`;
  Deepgram returns `metadata.model_info` with `arch` and `version`. "Which provider and model
  generated this?" is answerable from the response rather than only from what Unpocketed
  requested — and Deepgram's `version` is strictly more than §10's `modelId` can hold. Worth
  storing the returned model string, not the requested one, so a silently-upgraded provider model
  is visible.
- **§21's four states survive**, and for the first time they are checkable against a server.
  `queued` and `processing` both map to `transcribing`. But §21's "A failed transcription should
  be retryable" needs a caveat for `custom-openai` → Groq, where ASH makes retry unsatisfiable
  for up to an hour.
- **§10's `Transcript` survives v0.1 with a flat `text`.** 01 concluded it was "wrong as
  specified" *conditional on chunking*. Chunking is out, so **the condition does not trigger and
  no segment/offset model is needed.** The one addition is a nullable provider job reference
  (which may belong on a pending-transcription row rather than on `Transcript` itself) —
  additive, not a restructuring. **`src/types/index.ts` therefore needs no change to
  `Transcript.text`.** This is the ticket's open question answered: the consequence of flat `text`
  not surviving chunking is moot, because chunking does not happen.
- **§12's tension with §21 is resolved on size, but not on transport.** 01 found that §12's
  quality floor, §34's two-hour target and §21's "send original/correctly encoded audio" were not
  simultaneously satisfiable. With a 2.2 GB cap and native `.m4a`/AAC support at both providers,
  **all three are now satisfiable in principle, with the original file sent untouched** — which
  also removes the §3.2 pressure to transcode a throwaway copy for upload. That part of 01's
  contradiction is genuinely resolved by provider choice rather than compromise.
  **It is not resolved in practice:** per [§5.1](#51-removes-a-whole-workstream--which-workstream-exactly),
  sending the original still means one ~90 MB non-resumable POST over mobile uplink, so §21's
  upload step remains the least reliable link in the chain regardless of provider. §32 needs to
  say something true about that, and it is the residual risk this decision does not remove.
- **§8's lack of an audio-slicing capability stops being a gap.** No native module, no JS MP4
  parser, no unbudgeted PR7 scope. 01's flag for the roadmap can be closed.
- **`expo-audio`'s `LOW_QUALITY` `.3gp`/AMR-NB problem is narrower than 01 recorded.**
  AssemblyAI accepts `.amr` and `.3ga`, so that path is no longer a hard transcription failure —
  only a §12 quality failure. Still not a route to take; it just stops being a correctness
  landmine.

### Roadmap: PR7–PR9

- **PR7 — Initial transcription.** Ships AssemblyAI. Beyond §38's list: persist the provider job
  ref, resume polling on launch, jittered poll interval, and the `ProviderCapabilities` surface
  (needed here so PR8 can add `custom-openai` without reworking the interface). Exit condition
  unchanged and reachable.
- **PR8 — Bring-your-own provider.** Ships Deepgram **and** `custom-openai.ts` behind a declared
  cap with pre-flight refusal. §38's exit condition — "The same recording can be transcribed
  successfully by two different models/providers" — is met by AssemblyAI + Deepgram, and the
  escape hatch is a bonus rather than a dependency. **Chunking is not in this PR**, which is the
  substantive change to the roadmap's risk profile.
- **PR9 — Transcript ownership.** Unaffected. Flat `text` means TXT/Markdown/JSON export stays
  simple, and multiple transcripts per recording already carry provider+model attribution. §22's
  retranscription becomes the quality-comparison mechanism this document deliberately leans on.

### Map

The first *Not yet specified* entry — chunk assembly and partial failure — **evaporates**, per its
own contingency. The *Provider quota exhaustion under bring-your-own-key* entry **narrows to
Groq-via-`custom-openai` only**, since neither shipping provider has an audio-throughput ceiling;
it is now a disclosure problem, not a provider-selection one. The no-ORM decision's stated
revisit trigger does not fire.
